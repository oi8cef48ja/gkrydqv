Published on: 7/21/2026

How to prevent brain-eating amoeba infection?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Brain-eating amoeba (Naegleria fowleri) infections, though rare, are almost always fatal. To reduce your risk, avoid swimming, diving, or jumping into warm freshwater lakes, rivers, and hot springs—especially during summer months when water temperatures are high. Keep your head above water, use nose clips, and never stir up sediment in shallow warm water. For swimming pools and splash pads, ensure water is properly chlorinated and treated. When rinsing sinuses or using neti pots, only use distilled, sterile, or previously boiled water—never tap water.

Explanation

Brain-eating amoeba, known scientifically as Naegleria fowleri, is a rare but deadly organism found in warm freshwater. It can cause severe infections when water containing the amoeba enters the body through the nose.

Prevention Tips

Avoid Warm Freshwater: Try not to swim in warm freshwater lakes, rivers, or hot springs, especially during hot weather.

Use Properly Treated Water: Ensure water in pools and splash pads is treated with appropriate chemicals, like chlorine, to kill the amoeba.

Nasal Protection: Keep your head above water in potentially risky environments, or use nose clips to prevent water from entering your nose.

Avoid Stirring Up Sediment: If you are in a natural body of water, try not to disturb the sediment, as the amoeba can live in the bottom soil.

By following these steps, you can reduce your risk of infection from this dangerous organism. Always ensure that any water used for recreational purposes is properly treated and safe.
