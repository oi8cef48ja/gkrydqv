Reviewed by Tomas Diaz, MD

Emergency Medicine

Tingling in the eyelids can be caused by fatigue, nerve irritation, stress, or underlying conditions like migraines or dry eyes. It’s usually harmless but worth monitoring if it persists.

Fatigue or eye strain: Lack of sleep or too much screen time can lead to eye muscle fatigue, sometimes resulting in tingling or twitching. Stress or anxiety: High stress levels can trigger nerve-related symptoms, including tingling in sensitive areas like the eyelids. Dry eyes or irritation: If the eyes are dry or exposed to irritants (like wind, allergens, or contact lenses), the eyelids may feel tingly or uncomfortable. Migraine aura: Some people experience tingling around the eyes or face as part of a migraine episode. Trigeminal nerve sensitivity: This facial nerve controls sensation in and around the eye, and irritation can cause tingling or numbness. If tingling in your eyelids is persistent, accompanied by vision changes, pain, or other facial symptoms, it’s best to see a healthcare provider to rule out more serious causes.
