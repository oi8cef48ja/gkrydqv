Chest pain from fibromyalgia is typically chronic, often lasting months or years with fluctuating intensity. Known as costochondralgia or costochondritis, this musculoskeletal pain stems from inflammation where the ribs meet the breastbone, not from the heart itself. Flare-ups may be triggered by stress, poor sleep, weather changes, or physical overexertion, while symptoms often ease with rest, gentle stretching, and consistent fibromyalgia management.

Fibromyalgia is a chronic condition that affects many parts of the body, including causing chest pain. This chest pain does not usually come and go in a short period; instead, it can be a long-term discomfort. Many people with fibromyalgia experience chest pain along with widespread pain, and its intensity can change over time. Some days, the pain might feel worse during a flare-up, while other days it might be milder. Because fibromyalgia is a chronic condition, the chest pain may last for months or even years if it is not well-managed. If you're experiencing persistent chest pain or other concerning symptoms, you can use a free symptom checker to help understand what might be causing your discomfort. Treatment such as medications, gentle exercise, stress management, and other therapies can help lessen the pain and improve quality of life. It is important to work closely with a healthcare provider to find the best treatment plan for you, as each person's experience with fibromyalgia can be different. Remember that managing fibromyalgia is often about learning to cope with the symptoms over time while striving to reduce the frequency and severity of flare-ups.
