Why You Feel Exhausted on a Cleanse: Understanding Metabolic Stress

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Juice cleanses often cause fatigue because severe calorie restriction and the absence of protein, healthy fats, and electrolytes trigger metabolic stress. This leads to blood sugar crashes, hormonal shifts, muscle breakdown, and dehydration, leaving you drained.

Explanation

Many people turn to a juice cleanse seeking a quick reset or detox. However, it's common to experience low energy, fatigue, or even feeling downright exhausted within a day or two. Understanding the underlying causes—especially metabolic stress—can help you decide whether a cleanse is right for you and how to manage side effects if you choose to continue.

What Is a Juice Cleanse?

A juice cleanse typically involves replacing solid foods with fruit and vegetable juices for a set period, often 1–7 days. The idea is to flood your body with micronutrients while "giving your digestive system a break." In practice, many juice cleanses:

Provide 500–800 calories per day (far below most adult needs).

Are very low in protein and healthy fats.

Can lack essential electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium).

Often lead to rapid changes in blood sugar.

This drastic shift from a balanced diet to liquid-only intake triggers a range of metabolic and hormonal responses—some of which cause that familiar crash in energy.

Metabolic Stress: The Body's Reaction to Calorie Restriction

When your body senses a sudden drop in calories and nutrient variety, it responds by activating survival mechanisms:

Blood Sugar Roller Coaster• Low caloric intake leads to quickly falling blood glucose.• In response, liver glycogen stores release glucose to keep your brain and muscles fueled.• Within hours, those stores deplete, causing shakiness, dizziness, and fatigue.

Hormonal Shifts• Cortisol (a stress hormone) rises to help maintain blood sugar, which can leave you jittery or anxious.• Thyroid hormones (T3 and T4) may dip, slowing your metabolism to conserve energy.

Electrolyte Imbalance• Without sufficient sodium, potassium, and magnesium, nerve and muscle cells can misfire.• You may feel muscle cramps, headaches, weakness, or heart palpitations.

Muscle Breakdown• With little to no protein intake, your body can break down muscle tissue to supply amino acids for critical functions.• Loss of lean mass lowers resting metabolic rate, making you feel sluggish.

Dehydration Risk• Juices can be diuretic (especially green juices high in chlorophyll), leading to water loss.• Dehydration intensifies fatigue, headaches, and difficulty concentrating.

Common Symptoms of Low Energy on a Cleanse

You might notice some or all of these signs within 24–72 hours of starting a strict juice cleanse:

Profound tiredness and the urge to nap

Irritability or mood swings

Brain fog and difficulty focusing

Dizziness or lightheadedness when standing

Headaches, especially in the temples or behind the eyes

Muscle cramps or general weakness

Cold intolerance (feeling chilly even in warm weather)

Why Low Energy Happens on a Juice Cleanse

Too Few Calories • Most adults need 1,200–2,400 calories daily, depending on age, gender, and activity level.• Dropping below 800 calories disrupts basic functions like breathing, brain activity, and heart rate.

Lack of Protein and Fats • Protein is essential for muscle maintenance, immune function, and neurotransmitter production.• Healthy fats (omega-3s, monounsaturated fats) support hormone balance and sustained energy.

Blood Sugar Fluctuations • Juices often contain high sugar loads (fructose, glucose) that spike blood sugar, followed by rapid crashes.• These swings leave you feeling wired at first, then wiped out.

Electrolyte Depletion • Insufficient salt and minerals impairs muscle contraction and nerve signaling.• You may feel weak, dizzy, or experience heart palpitations.

Stress Response • Elevated cortisol can interfere with sleep quality, leading to daytime drowsiness.• Higher stress hormones may also promote water retention and bloating.

Tips to Minimize Fatigue (If You Insist on Cleansing)

If you decide a juice cleanse is worth trying, consider these adjustments to reduce metabolic stress and low energy:

Incorporate plant-based protein powders into your juices (pea, hemp, or soy protein).

Add a tablespoon of healthy fats (chia seeds, flaxseed oil, avocado) to slow sugar absorption.

Include vegetable-forward blends (cucumber, celery, spinach) to lower sugar content.

Drink at least 2–3 liters of water daily to counteract diuretic effects.

Sprinkle a pinch of sea salt into your juice to help maintain electrolytes.

Pack in more fiber-rich pulp or blend rather than strain, slowing digestion and stabilizing blood sugar.

Get extra rest—short naps or gentle stretching can ease muscle fatigue.

Limit high-intensity workouts; focus on walking, yoga, or light stretching instead.

Who Should Avoid or Modify a Juice Cleanse

Certain groups are especially vulnerable to the effects of a low-calorie juice regimen:

People with diabetes or blood sugar regulation issues

Those who are pregnant or breastfeeding

People with a history of eating disorders

Athletes or anyone with a very active lifestyle

Anyone taking prescription medications that require consistent nutrition

If you fall into one of these categories or start experiencing alarming symptoms—such as chest pain, severe dizziness, fainting, or confusion—stop the cleanse immediately.

Your health always comes first. A juice cleanse can feel rejuvenating for some, but it often comes with significant metabolic stress that leads to low energy and fatigue. Before committing:
