Can You Catch Tonsillitis From Someone With a Sore Throat?

Yes, tonsillitis is contagious when caused by infectious agents. Viruses (like the common cold, flu, or Epstein-Barr) and bacteria (such as Streptococcus) spread through coughs, sneezes, shared utensils, or close contact. If someone's sore throat stems from one of these infections, you can catch the underlying illness—which may develop into tonsillitis.

Symptoms overlap with many other conditions, from strep throat to mono to allergies, making it hard to know what you're actually dealing with. Guessing wrong can delay treatment or lead to unnecessary worry. Explanation

Tonsillitis can be spread through infections that cause sore throats, such as certain bacteria and viruses. When someone with a sore throat coughs, sneezes, or even talks, tiny droplets containing germs can be released into the air. If you breathe in these droplets or touch surfaces where they have landed and then touch your face, you might catch the infection.
