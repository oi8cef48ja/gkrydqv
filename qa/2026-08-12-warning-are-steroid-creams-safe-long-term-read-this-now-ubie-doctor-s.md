Are steroid creams safe for long-term use?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Steroid creams (topical corticosteroids) can be safe for long-term use when applied correctly and under medical supervision. However, prolonged use may cause side effects such as skin thinning, stretch marks, discoloration, or increased sensitivity. To minimize risks, doctors often recommend using the lowest effective potency, applying only to affected areas, and taking scheduled breaks between treatment cycles.

Explanation

Steroid creams, also known as topical corticosteroids, are often used to treat skin conditions like eczema and psoriasis. According to recent studies, these creams can be safe for long-term use, but there are some things to be careful about.

Possible Side Effects:

Skin Thinning: One of the most common concerns with long-term use is skin thinning. This can happen if the creams are used inappropriately or for too long.

Other Risks: There is also a small risk of developing skin issues like stretch marks or even skin cancer, although this is less common.

Guidelines for Safe Use:

Follow Doctor's Advice: Always use the cream as directed by a healthcare professional.

Monitor Skin Changes: Regularly check your skin for any changes and report them to your doctor.
