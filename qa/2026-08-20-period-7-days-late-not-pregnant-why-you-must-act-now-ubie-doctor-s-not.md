A period that's 7 days late with a negative pregnancy test taken 10–14 days after the missed date usually rules out pregnancy. In most cases, the delay reflects a temporary shift in your cycle caused by stress, hormonal changes, weight fluctuations, thyroid issues, PCOS, or lifestyle factors. If your period doesn't return soon or you notice other symptoms, it's worth investigating further.

Because irregular periods can stem from many overlapping causes, guessing isn't the best path forward. [Not Pregnant](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/blog/3165411447.html)

A period being 7 days late can happen for many reasons besides pregnancy. Sometimes stress, changes in sleep patterns, or fluctuations in weight and exercise routines can change your menstrual cycle. Hormonal imbalances, even temporary ones, might cause a delay. It's also possible to have a slightly irregular cycle, which is common for many women. Although the absence of pregnancy symptoms makes a positive result less likely, early pregnancy can sometimes be hard to detect before a missed period and the presence of other signs. If your period is late only once, you can wait a few more days and see if it starts naturally. You might also consider taking a home pregnancy test again if you remain unsure, especially if you test with your first morning urine, which is more reliable. If your period does not return or if you notice other changes or discomfort, you can use this free symptom checker to help identify what might be causing your symptoms, or contact your healthcare provider for a complete evaluation and to better understand your cycle.
