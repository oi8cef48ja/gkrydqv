Internal Medicine

Light bleeding one week after your period typically signals ovulation, when your ovary releases an egg. This mid-cycle spotting is caused by the natural hormonal shifts—specifically a brief drop in estrogen—that occur around egg release. It's usually light, brief, and considered normal.

However, bleeding between periods can sometimes point to other causes, such as hormonal imbalances, birth control effects, infections, fibroids, or thyroid issues. Because the causes range from harmless to conditions worth addressing, it's smart to understand what's driving your symptoms.

Explanation

[Signs Ubie](https://pepvoice.com/)

During the average menstrual cycle of 28 days, ovulation usually occurs about one week after menstruation ends. During ovulation, there may be slight bleeding due to fluctuations in female hormones. Recent change in hormonal contraception, stress or lifestyle changes, fibroids and infections can also cause bleeding less commonly. If you're experiencing unusual bleeding patterns, you can check your symptoms to better understand potential causes.
