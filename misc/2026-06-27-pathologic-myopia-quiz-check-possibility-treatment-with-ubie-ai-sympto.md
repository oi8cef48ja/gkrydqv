What is Pathologic Myopia?

Pathologic myopia is a type of farsightedness that causes the eyeball to elongate, resulting in high myopic refractive power. It is accompanied by degenerative changes in the eye and may lead to vision loss.

Typical Symptoms of Pathologic Myopia

Difficulty seeing due to poor color or light contrast

Partial loss of vision

Difficulty seeing on one side

Eyes point in different directions i.e. squint or cross-eyes

Changes in color perception

White area of the eyeball is puffy or swollen

Habit of watching things with the head slanted

Double vision

Diagnostic Questions for Pathologic Myopia

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Is it hard for you to distinguish subtle differences in color intensity or brightness?

Have you experienced partial loss of vision?

Are you currently experiencing vision loss in one eye?
