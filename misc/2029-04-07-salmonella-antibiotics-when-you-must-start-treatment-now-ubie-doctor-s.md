Salmonella infections typically do not require antibiotics. Most healthy people recover on their own within 4 to 7 days with rest and plenty of fluids. Antibiotics are generally reserved for high-risk groups, including infants, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems, where the infection is more likely to spread beyond the intestines and cause serious complications.

Because Salmonella symptoms—like diarrhea, fever, and stomach cramps—can overlap with many other conditions, it's important to understand what you're actually dealing with before deciding on next steps. Explanation

Most people with Salmonella infections do not need antibiotics. Their bodies can fight off the infection on their own. However, antibiotics might be recommended for certain people:

Young Children and Older Adults: Babies and older people might need antibiotics because their bodies can have a harder time fighting infections.

Weak Immune System: People whose immune systems don't work well, such as those with certain medical conditions, might need antibiotics.

Severe Symptoms: If someone has very bad symptoms, like a high fever or blood in their stool, a doctor might consider antibiotics.

In general, antibiotics are not given to everyone with Salmonella because they can sometimes make things worse by making the bacteria stay in the body longer.
