How does BlueChew Gold work?

BlueChew Gold works by reaching beyond the bloodflow-related effects of common ED medications, like Viagra and Cialis, and including two additional novel treatments that target ED through different mechanisms. Below, we explore each of these medications to determine either how they’ve been proven to help men with ED or how the research behind them indicates they should help with ED, given everything we know about the issue.

We’ll start with the generic versions of Viagra and Cialis, after which we’ll discuss apomorphine and oxytocin as ancillary therapies that could make BlueChew Gold the right choice for you.

Sildenafil

Sildenafil is the active ingredient in Viagra, which has been a frontline treatment for ED for nearly 30 years. It’s designed to inhibit the action of phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5), an enzyme responsible for degrading a compound called cGMP.13 cGMP builds up in response to sexual stimuli, resulting in increased blood flow to the penis. By preventing normal cGMP degradation, PDE5 inhibitors prime the system to create an erection in response to a stimulus. In most cases, the amount of PDE5 produced shortly after climax is sufficient to overcome the action of a PDE5 inhibitor, allowing an erection to subside normally after intercourse.

The first major study on its effectiveness, dating back to 1998, revealed that around 69% of attempts at intercourse for men with mild to moderate ED were successful after a single sildenafil dose, compared to just 22% with a placebo.14 Pooled analysis of a handful of 12-week follow-up studies saw up to 87% of patients reporting improved erections.6

So, for many men, sildenafil is plenty effective to improve their ED and sex lives. But it’s important to understand sildenafil’s time to onset and duration of action, as they differ significantly from tadalafil. Sildenafil takes about the same amount of time to start working as tadalafil (around 30-60 minutes), but it only lasts for 6-8 hours.15 By comparison, as we’ll discuss in a moment, tadalafil can last for more than 24 hours.7 Tadalafil can also be taken daily, whereas sildenafil is strictly an as-needed medication when taken for ED.

Tadalafil

Tadalafil is the other PDE5 inhibitor in BlueChew Gold, and while it works similarly to sildenafil, its duration of action and collateral inhibition of other PDE enzymes are quite different. Specifically, for most men, tadalafil works for 18-36 hours, which is much more convenient if you’re anticipating the need for erectile assistance but not completely sure when it will arise.7 You don’t have to worry as much about dose timing, and you can enjoy the duration of the evening — however long it might last — without concern about taking a second dose.

This added convenience is why a survey study of men and their partners using either sildenafil or tadalafil revealed a significant preference for tadalafil over sildenafil.16 As an added convenience, tadalafil has been shown to work as a daily medication, often at slightly lower doses than the as-needed variety. That allows men to take it consistently without worrying about remembering to take it or to bring it with them when they might need it.

Apomorphine

Apomorphine is a dopaminergic agonist that has often been studied in cases of Parkinson's disease. Rather than affecting blood flow like a PDE5 inhibitor, apomorphine impacts dopamine receptors in the brain, one of which has been linked with erectile performance.17 It has been shown to be effective in large clinical trials at a dose range of 2-6mg, with 3mg and 4mg doses proving the most effective.18

Additional research to corroborate these findings and establish how apomorphine might interact with PDE5 inhibitors would be welcome, but it has not yet been conducted.

It’s important to note that apomorphine isn’t directly related to morphine in any meaningful way. There are no similar withdrawal symptoms or addiction risks.

Oxytocin

Oxytocin is a hormone produced in instances of social bonding, not least of all during courtship and early parenting, earning it the nickname “the love hormone.”19 Of the four medications in Gold, oxytocin has the least amount of research looking at its impacts on erectile health. That said, it also has the most amount of research looking at its role in mental health, which turns out to be a major driver of ED.

In fact, up to 40% of ED cases have been shown to be psychogenic in origin, which is to say that they’re rooted in things like depression or anxiety.3 Add to that the number of men who suffer impacts to their mental health as a result of organic ED (i.e., ED from blood flow problems or other physiological issues), and it becomes immediately clear that medication to improve mental health can make a significant difference for men with any kind of ED.20

Mental health research has revealed that oxytocin has the potential to treat depression, anxiety, and several other mood disorders, regardless of ED status.5 So adding it alongside two PDE5 inhibitors and a dopaminergic agonist could, at least in theory, help tackle ED from more angles for more men than traditional monotherapy or even other combination therapies could.

And that market force has also spurred the creation of alternatives to some of these medications that act similarly but with lower risk.

So, let’s take a quick look at the potential side effects of each medication in BlueChew Gold to see how the literature characterizes their risk profiles.

Sildenafil

In a four-year study of nearly 1,000 men, the most common treatment-related side effects from sildenafil were headache and dyspepsia at roughly 1% each.21 After that, rhinitis affected around 0.6% of the patient population, flushing impacted around 0.5%, and dizziness affected around 0.3%.

Sildenafil has also been shown to cause a color-processing problem in a small percentage of users, arising from the collateral inhibition of another phosphodiesterase enzyme, PDE6. In that same four-year study, vision disturbances were seen in just 0.4% of patients. It was a flexible dose study, with doses ranging from 25mg to 100mg. Studies dedicated to examining sildenafil’s vision side effects typically use the higher 100mg dose. One such study reported visual disturbances in more than 76% of patients.22 However, it was a much smaller study, with just 17 participants.

Tadalafil

Tadalafil doesn’t cause the same vision disturbances seen with sildenafil, but it has its own side-effect profile, with some similarities worth noting. A long-term tadalafil safety study showed an increased risk of several side effects compared to sildenafil, including headache (15.8%), dyspepsia (11.8%), nasopharyngitis (11.4%), and back pain (8.2%).23

Apomorphine

Much of the safety data concerning apomorphine comes to us from Parkinson’s disease trials, where it’s often delivered as an injection or infusion. Some trials, however, have used sublingual administration not unlike the lozenges and tablets from leading ED medication providers. In infusion settings, nausea was the most common side effect.24

In apomorphine ED studies, nausea also rears its head in about 3% of patients, but it appears to subside with subsequent doses, indicating increased tolerance over time.18 Dizziness, yawning, flushing, and sweating were among the other noted side effects, but never in more than 4% of the patient population.

Oxytocin

Oral oxytocin doesn’t appear to be associated with very many adverse effects, though research into intranasal administration or infusion during childbirth has exhibited sometimes serious effects.25 One large review of intranasal oxytocin, at doses roughly one-fifth of those given orally in BlueChew Gold, saw a wide range of adverse effects, including GI upset, soft stool, dizziness, lightheadedness, euphoria, hallucinations, insomnia, and aggression.26

However, another study comparing intranasal oxytocin to oral sublingual oxytocin noted drastic differences in action, indicating the possibility that the side effects of one route of administration could likewise be very different.27

Taken together, these drugs appear to be mostly safe for most people, with your doctor’s consultation standing as an effective guard against potential concerns or contraindications with other medications or conditions.

Alternatives to BlueChew Gold

As we touched on above, BlueChew Gold’s unique combination of prescription medications makes it a potentially very effective choice for men who’ve struggled to find success taking just one medication at a time. But if you’re new to ED treatments, you might not want to start with something this strong. There are also some therapies you can seek alongside BlueChew’s various treatments, including Gold, to help maximize your results.

Let’s take a look at some of those alternative or complementary options:

Monotherapies

For most men new to ED treatments, monotherapies are going to be the starting point. Drugs like sildenafil and tadalafil have proven effective for the vast majority of users, with minimal side effects, plus options to switch to other monotherapies like avanafil or vardenafil should side effects from the others become a problem. BlueChew also offers such monotherapies in its chewable tablet format.

Acoustic shockwave therapy

Acoustic shockwave therapy involves a powerful concussive or electromagnetic force that creates pressure waves capable of penetrating target tissues in the penis to stimulate improved blood flow and vascular repair.28 We'd recommend seeking it through a urologist for in-office treatment, as at-home devices currently on the market are typically either unsafe or ineffective.

Testosterone treatments and supplements

Some cases of ED can be traced back to low testosterone, which impacts erectile performance, libido, and much more.29 Testosterone replacement — either as injectable/oral testosterone or through other medications that can influence levels — is necessary in some cases, but testosterone boosters like Innerbody Labs Testosterone Support can also help.

Penis pumps

Penis pumps mechanically draw blood into the penis using vacuum pressure. They can be cumbersome to use, but they’re effective for men whose ED is relatively unresponsive to ED medications, especially those who’ve undergone a radical prostatectomy.30
