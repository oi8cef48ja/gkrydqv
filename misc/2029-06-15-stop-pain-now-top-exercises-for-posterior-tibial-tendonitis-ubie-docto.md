Internal Medicine

Posterior tibial tendonitis can be effectively managed with targeted exercises. Eccentric strengthening, in which the tendon lengthens under load, helps rebuild tendon strength and resilience. Specific foot and ankle movements—such as heel raises, resisted foot inversion, and arch-lifting exercises—reduce pain, restore stability, and support proper foot alignment during walking and daily activity.

Explanation

Posterior tibial tendonitis can be improved with specific exercises that focus on strengthening the tendon and surrounding muscles. Research suggests that eccentric exercises, where the muscle lengthens while under tension, are particularly beneficial. These exercises help to strengthen the tendon and improve its function.
