1. What Is Apple Cider Vinegar (ACV)?

Apple cider vinegar is made by fermenting crushed apples, turning their natural sugars first into alcohol and then into acetic acid. This acid gives ACV its sour taste and many of its proposed health effects. Unfiltered or "raw" ACV often contains a cloudy substance called "the mother," which is a mix of beneficial bacteria, yeast, and proteins.

Key components of ACV:

Acetic acid: Main active compound, responsible for vinegar's acidity.

Trace minerals: Potassium, magnesium, and small amounts of others.

Antioxidants: Polyphenols derived from apples.

5. Possible Risks and Side Effects

No supplement is free of potential downsides. When overused or undiluted, ACV may cause:

Tooth enamel erosion: Acetic acid is acidic enough to weaken enamel over time.

Throat or esophageal irritation: Sipping straight vinegar can burn sensitive tissues.

Digestive discomfort: Some experience nausea, bloating, or acid reflux.

Drug interactions: May affect medications for diabetes, heart disease, or osteoporosis.

Always mention any regular medications to your doctor before adding ACV to your routine.

6. Safe Usage Guidelines

To minimize risks, follow these doctor-approved tips:

Dilute generously• Mix 1–2 teaspoons (5–10 mL) of ACV in at least 8 ounces (240 mL) of water or herbal tea.

Limit frequency• Stick to once or twice per day, ideally before meals.

Use a straw• Bypass teeth to protect enamel.

Rinse mouth afterward• Swish plain water to neutralize residual acid.

Choose high-quality ACV• Look for "raw," unfiltered options with "the mother."

If you experience persistent throat discomfort or digestive issues, stop ACV and speak to your doctor.
