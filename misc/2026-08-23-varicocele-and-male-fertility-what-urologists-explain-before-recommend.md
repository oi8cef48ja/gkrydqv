What Is a Varicocele?

A varicocele is similar to a varicose vein you might see in the leg, but it occurs in the scrotum:

Veins above the testicle (the pampiniform plexus) become enlarged or dilated.

This can lead to impaired blood flow and increased scrotal temperature.

Most varicoceles develop on the left side, though they can be bilateral.

Many men don't notice symptoms. When present, they may include:

A dull, dragging pain or discomfort in the scrotum.

A "bag of worms" sensation when touching the scrotum.

Visible or palpable enlarged veins.

Impact on Semen Parameters

Clinical studies show men with varicoceles often have:

Lower sperm count (oligospermia).

Reduced sperm motility (asthenospermia).

Abnormal sperm shape (teratospermia).

Improving these parameters can translate into higher chances of natural conception or success with assisted reproductive techniques.

Non-Surgical Management

Before jumping to repair, doctors often recommend conservative measures:

Wearing supportive underwear or an athletic supporter.

Taking over-the-counter anti-inflammatories (e.g., ibuprofen) for discomfort.

Monitoring with regular physical exams and ultrasound imaging.

Lifestyle adjustments: limiting heavy lifting and avoiding prolonged standing.

These steps can relieve minor symptoms and help determine if surgery is truly necessary.

Surgical and Non-Surgical Repair Options

If conservative measures fail and fertility or pain concerns persist, repair options include:

Microscopic (Microsurgical) Varicocelectomy

Gold standard with the lowest complication and recurrence rates.

Performed through a small incision in the groin or lower abdomen using a surgical microscope.

Veins are tied off, rerouting blood through healthy vessels.

Laparoscopic Varicocelectomy

Minimally invasive, using small abdominal incisions and a camera.

Requires general anesthesia.

Similar success rates to microscopic repair.

Percutaneous Embolization

Interventional radiology procedure.

A coil or sclerosant is inserted via a vein in the groin or neck to block abnormal veins.

Avoids incisions but has slightly higher recurrence risk.

Your urologist will discuss the pros and cons of each approach based on your anatomy, symptoms, and fertility goals.

Expected Benefits and Timeline

After varicocele repair, many men experience:

Improved semen parameters in 3–6 months.

Progressive increases in sperm count, motility, and morphology.

Potential for natural conception; cumulative pregnancy rates range from 30–60% in couples trying to conceive.

It's important to have realistic expectations: not every man will achieve pregnancy naturally after repair, and additional fertility treatments may still be needed.

Risks and Complications

Like all procedures, varicocele repair carries some risks:

Hydrocele formation (fluid buildup around the testicle).

Injury to the testicular artery (rare with microsurgery).

Recurrence of the varicocele in up to 5–15% of cases, depending on technique.

Pain or bruising at incision sites.

Urologists weigh these small risks against the potential fertility benefits and your personal situation.

When to Speak to a Doctor

Varicoceles are rarely life-threatening, but any of the following warrant prompt medical attention:

Sudden, severe scrotal pain or swelling.

Signs of infection: fever, redness, or warmth in the scrotum.

Testicular lumps or rapid changes in size.

Any symptom causing significant worry or impacting daily life.

Always "speak to a doctor" about anything that could be serious or life-threatening.

Conclusion
