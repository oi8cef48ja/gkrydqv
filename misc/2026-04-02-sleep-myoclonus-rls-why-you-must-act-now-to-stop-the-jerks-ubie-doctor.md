Can sleep myoclonus occur alongside restless legs syndrome (RLS)?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep myoclonus and restless legs syndrome (RLS) can occur together. Both conditions involve involuntary movements during sleep and share overlapping neurological mechanisms, making it common for people to experience symptoms of both simultaneously.

Key points:

Sleep myoclonus causes sudden muscle jerks during sleep or as you fall asleep

RLS creates an uncomfortable urge to move the legs, often worse at night

Both conditions can disrupt sleep quality and may respond to similar treatments

Iron deficiency, medications, and nervous system factors may contribute to either or both
