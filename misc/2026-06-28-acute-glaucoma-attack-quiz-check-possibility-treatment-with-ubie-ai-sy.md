What is Acute Glaucoma Attack?

A condition caused by high pressures in the eyeball, more common in people with farsightedness. Persons of Black, Asian, or Hispanic descent are at higher risk. The high pressures can cut off blood flow to the eye. Prompt medical attention is needed to prevent permanent blindness.

Typical Symptoms of Acute Glaucoma Attack

Difficulty seeing due to poor color or light contrast

Bloodshot eyes

Redness of the whites of the eyes

Eye pain or discomfort

Headache with pain behind the eyes

Tearing from one eye

Headache

Eyes are red, itchy or painful

Diagnostic Questions for Acute Glaucoma Attack

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Is it hard for you to distinguish subtle differences in color intensity or brightness?

Do you have bloodshot (red) eyes?

Are the whites of your eyes red?

Do you have any eye pain or discomfort?

Do you have headaches with pain behind your eyes?

Treatment of Acute Glaucoma Attack

This requires emergency medical care. Avoid staying in a dark room or lying down as this can worsen the condition. In the first stage, medicines to lower eye pressures will be given in the form of eyedrops, oral medicine, and medicine given through a needle in the vein. In the second stage, a laser procedure or surgery may be needed to permanently fix the problem.

A.

Progressively dimming or narrowed side vision often points to glaucoma, a silent optic nerve disease usually related to eye pressure; a sudden painful red eye with blurred vision, halos, headache, or nausea can signal acute angle closure that needs emergency care. Treatment focuses on lowering eye pressure with prescription drops, sometimes oral meds, laser therapy, or surgery, plus consistent follow up; lost vision cannot be restored, so early diagnosis is key and there are several factors to weigh, with important details on testing, risks, and next steps below.
