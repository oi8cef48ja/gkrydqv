I love to incorporate alternating-rep exercises into my training routine.

When you alternate reps—or continuously switch which side of your body is performing the exercise—you can use heavier loads and keep your muscles’ under tension for longer than you could if you were doing bilateral or simultaneous reps with both sides.

Alternating reps is also a fantastic way to shore up weaknesses and to fix imbalances between sides. Everyone has a strong and a weak side. While you don’t consciously try to make one arm stronger or one leg more powerful than the other, it can happen when you only perform bilateral movements like the bench press or barbell squat.

Since they work your limbs at the same exact time, your dominant side overpowers your weaker side. But alternating reps forces both sides to work independently of one another, allowing them to get stronger together.

You can also intensify alternating-rep movements by switching up your starting point. For example, it’s harder to alternate from the bottom position of a chest press than the the top position because your “resting” side is still under tension in a fully stretched position.

By just changing where you start the weight, you can also make the exercise more challenging without ever increasing the weight. This is super helpful when you have limited equipment or minimal weight selections in a home or hotel gym setting.
